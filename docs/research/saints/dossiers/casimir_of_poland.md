# Saint Casimir

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `casimir_of_poland`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `casimir_of_poland`, `saint_casimir`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Casimir; Casimir Jagiellon; Kazimierz Jagiellończyk; Kazimieras
- Feast date and calendar scope: March 4; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: Polish-Lithuanian historical controls support 1458-1484, royal formation, the failed 1471 Hungarian campaign, and substantial administration from 1481 into 1483. October 3 and October 5 birth dates and second-son and third-son descriptions conflict, so only the year is user-facing. A formal chastity vow is not documentary fact. The secure 1602 cult recognition is not collapsed into a disputed claim of a surviving 1521 canonization bull.

## Uncertainties and conflicts

- Sources disagree on an October 3 or October 5 birth date and on Casimir's birth order. The profile uses only 1458.
- The 1471 campaign is secure, but a fully developed narrative that Casimir personally halted it from moral scruple is not.
- The early Ferreri tradition is valuable hagiographic evidence, not a contemporaneous administrative record.
- A 1521 Leo X bull is not securely extant; the profile does not state that canonization date as settled.
- Severe pulmonary illness is secure; tuberculosis remains a probable identification, and fasting is not named as a cause.

## Content review

The factual review checked the Hungarian chronology, Casimir's age, documented administration, death and burial, early devotional genre, cult dispute, and decree-level patronage. It rejects civic withdrawal, nationalist ownership, unsafe fasting, unsupported medical causation, hymn authorship, and later apparition claims. Retained `patronage` and `symbols` values have both compatibility support tokens and granular source keys.

## Theological review

Christ's servant authority governs the profile. Chastity is a free vocation and not contempt for marriage or embodiment. Prayer bears fruit in justice and attention to vulnerable people rather than escape from duty. Polish-Lithuanian devotion is directed toward communion and peace, not political triumph.
