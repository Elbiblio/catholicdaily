## Inquisition and institutional-harm safeguard

The Holy See's own institutional history explicitly places Ghislieri in senior Inquisition leadership and describes a judicial system that could condemn, use torture under its law, and hand people to civil authorities for execution. The profile neither personalizes every institutional act without a case record nor diffuses responsibility into anonymous context. Leadership, reinforcement of the institution, and its human consequences remain visible.

## Jewish, Protestant, and confessional-violence safeguard

- The 1569 Jewish expulsions, property burdens, commercial restrictions, and the testimony of an independent Jewish reference are present in the main biography.
- Pius's support for open war against French Huguenots is not reduced to generic “foreign policy.”
- His policy toward Elizabeth I is assessed in light of increased danger for already persecuted English Catholics.
- Context explains a sixteenth-century confessional state; it does not justify violated conscience, imprisonment, torture, expulsion, or killing.
- The prayer remembers harmed Jews and Christians and asks institutional repair rather than a devotional bypass.

## Liturgy, Lepanto, and canonization safeguard

The 1570 Missal is a major historical achievement but not the end of liturgical development; Paul VI's constitution and Francis's address make continuity and later reform explicit. Lepanto is recorded as a naval event and part of Rosary reception without making defeated peoples a prop for Catholic triumph. Canonization receives Pius's holiness but does not canonize the Inquisition, anti-Jewish law, confessional force, or every papal judgment.

## Rejected claims and editorial exclusions

- Rejected a profile centered only on austere piety, Trent, Rosary, and Lepanto.
- Rejected euphemisms that hide torture, execution, expulsion, or war inside “defense of orthodoxy.”
- Rejected the overclaim that Pius personally ordered every torture or execution permitted by the institution he led.
- Rejected a frozen-forever account of the 1570 Missal or a suggestion that later Roman reform betrayed his achievement.
- Rejected assigning divine approval to military victory or treating Ottoman opponents as faceless enemies of holiness.
- Rejected presentism that ignores historical structures, and rejected contextualism that erases victims.
- Rejected patronage and symbol arrays because no exact formal compatibility claim was needed and verified.

## Theological review

The guide measures authority by Christ's service and refuses to equate correct doctrine or canonization with morally licit means. Prayer and reform are honored; coercion and confessional violence are judged as harms. The practice requires listening to those affected by policy, transparent correction, and accountability. Prayer remembers specific victim communities and asks repentance, repair, conscience safeguards, and peace.
