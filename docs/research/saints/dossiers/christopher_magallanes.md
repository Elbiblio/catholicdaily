## Full roster and representative cases

The priests are Cristóbal Magallanes Jara, Agustín Caloca Cortés, José María Robles Hurtado, David Galván Bermúdez, Justino Orona Madrigal, Atilano Cruz Alvarado, Román Adame Rosales, Julio Álvarez Mendoza, Pedro Esqueda Ramírez, Rodrigo Aguilar Alemán, Tranquilino Ubiarco Robles, Jenaro Sánchez Delgadillo, José Isabel Flores Varela, Sabas Reyes Salazar, Toribio Romo González, Luis Batis Sáinz, Mateo Correa Magallanes, Pedro de Jesús Maldonado Lucero, Jesús Méndez Montoya, David Uribe Velasco, Margarito Flores García, and Miguel de la Mora de la Mora. The laymen are Manuel Morales, Salvador Lara Puente, and David Roldán Lara.

The cases span more than two decades. The decretal records David Galván killed in Guadalajara on January 30, 1915. Its entry for Pedro Maldonado says 1927, but the dedicated Vatican biography records his beating on February 10, 1937, and death in a Chihuahua hospital on February 11. That direct official biography controls the erroneous year while the discrepancy remains documented.

## Martyrdom, violence, and collective-blame safeguard

- Formal canonization evidence establishes a group of twenty-five with distinct martyrdom histories or cases examined within the group cause; without procedural proof, the profile does not claim twenty-five separately constituted individual causes. The group was not a combat unit, and the Cristero War itself was not canonized.
- State restrictions, arrests, executions, and counterinsurgency violence are named without blaming all public servants, soldiers, or Mexican people.
- Armed Catholic resistance is distinguished from priests and civilians; rebel violence against Catholic civilians and institutions is not hidden.
- Christopher's rejection of armed violence is not projected onto every companion, and no companion's cause is used to sanctify insurgency.
- Martyrdom never requires avoidable exposure, romanticizes death, or pressures contemporary believers to neglect safeguarding. Forgiveness rejects revenge while allowing truth, accountability, and repair.
