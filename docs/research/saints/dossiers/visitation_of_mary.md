## Uncertainties and conflicts

- “Cousin” is familiar devotional shorthand, but the reviewed Scripture page supports only a family relationship; the profile says relative.
- Luke does not specify Mary's companions, route, travel duration, or the pregnancies' medical circumstances. All such detail is omitted.
- Marialis Cultus presents charitable assistance as the liturgy's interpretation of the visit; the profile does not claim Luke explicitly states Mary's motive.
- The leaping child functions within Luke's Spirit-filled recognition scene. It is not used as medical evidence or a shortcut for a contemporary political argument.
- The feast's medieval history belongs to liturgical reception and is not merged into the biblical event as though it supplied new eyewitness detail.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Pregnancy is honored with dignity, rest, consent, practical support, and qualified medical care; risky travel or exhausting service is never prescribed as holiness.
- The profile does not assume Mary traveled alone and does not pressure pregnant people or caregivers to imitate narrative silence.
- Help must respond to a person's stated needs, respect family circumstances, and include safeguarding or clinical support when appropriate.
- The Magnificat forms solidarity with lowly and hungry people without using them as illustrations or reducing Catholic social responsibility to a partisan slogan.
