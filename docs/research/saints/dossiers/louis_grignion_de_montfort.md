## Marian devotion, servitude language, and safeguarding review

- Jesus Christ is the only Savior and final object of self-gift. Mary points to him and cannot replace Scripture, sacramental life, the Holy Spirit, responsible conscience, or Church accountability.
- Servitude and slavery language is identified as an early-modern theological metaphor and immediately translated as voluntary baptismal self-gift to Christ within the freedom of God's children.
- The metaphor never authorizes human ownership, domination, forced obedience, manipulation, abuse, secrecy, financial exploitation, isolation, or surrender of safeguarding rights.
- Devotional commitments must be free, proportionate, reviewable, and compatible with family, health, work, and ecclesial responsibilities.
- Fear-based promises, scrupulosity, magical guarantees, severe penance, and exhaustion are not proposed as practices. A person facing control or abuse is directed to stop and seek appropriate pastoral and safeguarding help.

## Theological review

The guide follows the primary text and formal papal reception in making Christ the sole Savior and ultimate end. Consecration is baptismal, voluntary, and ordered to mature freedom and charity. Marian devotion cannot bypass Jesus, Scripture, sacraments, conscience, or safeguarding. Historical servitude language is not normalized as modern interpersonal obedience. Service of poor neighbors requires consent and no devotional pressure.
