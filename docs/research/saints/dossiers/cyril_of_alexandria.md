## Uncertainties and conflicts

- Birth is approximate; no age is calculated.
- Nestorius and Cyril became polarized saint/heretic figures. Modern study requires careful reconstruction rather than inherited caricature.
- Ephesus's doctrinal reception is distinguished from the disputed opening before John's delegation, rival council, imperial involvement, and punishment.
- Socrates is near-contemporary but not a neutral transcript. The scale and permanence of Jewish expulsion are disputed; coercion and dispossession are still named.
- Hypatia was murdered by a Christian mob led by Peter the lector. No reviewed evidence shows Cyril issued an order, and no such allegation is made.
- Later anti-violence statements complicate Cyril's trajectory but do not cancel victims or institutional accountability.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Anti-Jewish rhetoric, collective blame, synagogue seizure, expulsion, and dispossession cannot be defended as protection of doctrine.
- Doctrinal disputes require accurate quotation, fair representation, participation, appeal, and nonretaliation.
- Crowds, clergy, or governments never gain permission to threaten or harm an opponent in Christ's name.
- Reporting incitement and protecting targeted people take priority over institutional reputation.
- Reconciliation language may be sought without forcing victims or minorities to minimize harm.
