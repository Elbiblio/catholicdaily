# Chair of Saint Peter

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `chair_of_saint_peter`
- Profile kind: observance
- Celebration IDs: `chair_of_saint_peter`, `the_chair_of_saint_peter_apostle`
- Canonical name and aliases: Chair of Saint Peter; Feast of the Chair of Saint Peter the Apostle
- Feast date and calendar scope: February 22; feast in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: This is an observance, not an additional biography or lifespan for Peter. The theological claim about Petrine teaching, pastoral care, and unity is distinct from the provenance of the wooden object in Saint Peter's Basilica. The official archive and scholarly control describe a composite throne with strong Carolingian associations and later medieval relic devotion; they do not establish it as Peter's first-century furniture. General patronage claims and a settled Antioch-origin narrative were rejected.

## Uncertainties and conflicts

- The feast is ancient, but the reviewed pages do not justify choosing a precise third- or fourth-century starting date. The profile simply gives the established February 22 observance.
- Official archive reporting presents competing dates and arrangements for the ivory panels and anticipates further scientific study. The profile says their dating is debated.
- The physical chair has devotional importance and a documented medieval history, but that history must not be collapsed into the theological claim about Petrine ministry.
- The keys and cathedra are source-established symbols; generic symbols and patronage were omitted.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Authority is presented as service answerable to Christ, never as immunity from review, coercion of conscience, or permission to conceal wrongdoing.
- Communion is not equated with institutional silence. Legitimate safeguarding complaints require safe reporting, protection from retaliation, and competent response.
- Historical humility about a material relic is not hostility to devotion; it prevents faith from depending on an archaeological claim the evidence cannot bear.
- No artificial lifespan or personal biography has been assigned to the observance.

## Theological review

- Christ remains the chief Shepherd and source of the commission; Peter's ministry neither replaces Christ nor owns the Church.
- Matthew 16 is read with John 21 and 1 Peter 5 so that keys, love, and non-dominating care interpret one another.
- Petrine unity is described as communion in confessed faith and merciful truth, not centralized domination.
- The profile distinguishes doctrine and liturgical meaning from the contingent provenance of a physical object.
- Prayer for pastors includes accountability and protection of people who report harm.
