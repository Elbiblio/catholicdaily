## Mission-record and martyr safeguard

- Chanel's diary and letters are privileged over later narrative arrangement, while the loss of the second diary volume is acknowledged.
- Niuliki and other Futunans are named as hosts, protectors, political actors, converts, opponents, and interpreters rather than one undifferentiated population.
- Chiefly politics and religious change are presented as locally meaningful, not primitive obstruction to an inevitable European project.
- Later martyr interpretation is honored as ecclesial memory without claiming that every motive, speech, or death detail is secure.
- The profile rejects hostile ethnic stereotypes, including the dated language in one reference entry.

## Cross-cultural and colonial-power safeguard

The guide distinguishes Chanel's patient conduct and objection to destroyed ancestral objects from the wider mission structure. The Marists arrived with foreign organization, material support, and European connections that became entangled with French imperial action and denominational rivalry. Aid is never presented as a legitimate conversion tool. Spiritual imitation asks users to listen, seek consent, strengthen local leadership, and examine who controls resources and narrative.

## Rejected claims and editorial exclusions

- Rejected the legacy estimate “about 38 years”; the elapsed age was 37.
- Rejected any hostile description of Futunans as savage, fickle, or incapable of reasoned agency.
- Rejected the compressed claim that one chief's fear of one son's conversion fully explains the killing.
- Rejected exact graphic death mechanics and speeches where later hagiographic retelling outruns the surviving record.
- Rejected an instant-conversion or “blood alone converted the island” narrative that erases later missionaries, Futunan political change, and French naval power.
- Rejected the conclusion that Chanel's personal gentleness makes the mission structurally free of colonial or material inequality.
- Rejected symbols and any patronage beyond the formally attested exact token `Oceania`.

## Theological review

Martyrdom is honored without treating violence or imperial expansion as providential instruments. Mission is framed through Christ's sending of dependent guests, free witness, service, repentance, and respect for conscience. Futunan cultures and decisions remain morally significant. The prayer names mission-linked contempt and empire, asks healing, and does not glorify death or demand conversion through aid.
