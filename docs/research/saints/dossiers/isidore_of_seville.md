## Anti-Judaism and attribution review

- “Anti-Jewish” is used for Isidore's theology and policy influence; “antisemitic” is avoided where it might blur the specific religious, legal, and seventh-century setting, without softening the harm.
- The profile neither excuses Isidore as a simple opponent of forced conversion nor assigns every council canon to him alone. It states his presidency and the council's contradictory coercive policy.
- Jewish people are not reduced to an object lesson for Christian improvement. The prayer names harm to Jewish people and asks forgiveness and repair.
- Compilation is described by the standards of the work's genre while still teaching modern readers to attribute, verify, and correct. Extensive borrowing is not mislabeled as modern plagiarism, nor celebrated as original authorship.
- Doctor-of-the-Church status is retained as formal ecclesial recognition without implying impeccability in scholarship, policy, or moral judgment.

## Theological review

Christ as truth is the center of learning and correction. Preserving knowledge is ordered to service, not status. The profile distinguishes doctrinal conviction from coercion and contempt; forced belief and child separation are not sanctified by council authority. Veneration of Isidore permits truthful judgment, repentance for Christian anti-Judaism, solidarity with Jewish people, and repair of inherited harm.
