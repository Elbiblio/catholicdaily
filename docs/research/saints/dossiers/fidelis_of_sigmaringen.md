## Confessional violence and coercion safeguard

- Reformed Christians are not described collectively as murderers, fanatics, heretics, or enemies of Christ.
- Fidelis's killing is condemned without romanticizing the attack, reproducing graphic detail, or treating death-seeking as courageous.
- Austrian occupation and the coercive religious mandate are named; Catholic policy is not sanitized because Fidelis was later canonized.
- Neither grievance nor state authority justifies murder, forced worship, expulsion, or manipulation of conscience.
- Old polemical narratives are used as evidence about reception and chronology only after their rhetoric is rejected.

## Copyright and media decision

All sources are summarized in original language. No reported dialogue, graphic death description, extended translation, or liturgical text is reproduced. No image is included because no file-specific creator, license, source page, credit line, and derivative status were reviewed.

## Content review

The factual pass checked the Mark Roy identity, approximate birth year, legal doctorates and practice, 1612 Capuchin entry, ordination and leadership, the Austrian occupation, Prättigau mission, mandate authorship, uprising, April 24 death, canonization, and lawyer patronage. It rejected an impossible 1611 mission date, collective Protestant blame, graphic details, a legendary final debate, inflammatory rhetoric, generic persecution patronage, and a distinct symbol.

## Theological review

The profile condemns both murder and coercive Catholic policy. Martyr memory does not canonize every institutional action, erase Reformed suffering, or make state pressure an instrument of evangelization. Christ-centered witness protects conscience, seeks peace, tells the truth about one's own community, and uses law to serve people with less power.
