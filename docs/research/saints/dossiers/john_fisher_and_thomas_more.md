## Uncertainties and conflicts

- There is no appropriate joint Wikidata entity or combined lifespan; individual identities are verified separately.
- More's silence, statutory interpretation, testimony attributed to Richard Rich, political motive, and trial procedure require legal-historical analysis rather than a simplified courtroom legend.
- More's coercive heresy policy is real. Particular personal-torture accusations are contested and are not repeated as fact.
- Fisher and More opposed one form of coercive supremacy, but this does not turn every earlier judgment or polemic into a model.
- The formal patronage of statesmen and politicians belongs to More alone and is excluded from the group array.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Conscience must be formed through truth, Scripture, Church teaching, competent counsel, duties, and the impact on other people.
- Martyrdom is not sought. Lawful safety, silence, appeal, counsel, documentation, and protection of dependants may be faithful.
- No one should use the saints to pressure a person to remain in abuse, refuse legal help, or make a family bear avoidable danger.
- Religious freedom and due process apply to opponents; coercion cannot become holy because a favored authority uses it.
- Historical criticism and acknowledgment of More's coercive actions are compatible with honoring his canonized witness.
