- Feast date and calendar scope: April 13; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: The Holy See registry identifies Martin as the seventy-fourth pope from Todi and gives July 5, 649 to September 16, 655. The New Catholic Encyclopedia notes that the pontificate may be ended administratively at deportation in 653 or biographically at death in 655. Birth date and year are unknown, so the unsupported legacy 598 is removed. Vatican News confirms April 13 for the present commemoration despite older reference calendars listing other dates.

## Custody, martyrdom, and institutional-power review

- Arrest, deprivation, humiliation, imprisonment, and exile are named as abuse of dignity rather than a desirable road to holiness.
- Martin's refusal of violent resistance is honored without requiring vulnerable people to remain in danger or waive lawful protection.
- The profile separates the Christological claim, synod authority, imperial unity policy, papal independence, and treason allegation. It does not collapse every participant into a simple believer or persecutor.
- Martyr language describes faithful witness under lethal mistreatment; it does not make captors' conduct sacramental, necessary, or worthy of imitation.

## Theological review

The profile centers the one Christ, fully divine and fully human, and explains human willing without depicting two persons. Conscience is formed by truth, prayer, collaboration, evidence, and concern for others rather than factional defiance. The state may not erase doctrine by force, and church office does not erase political complexity. Abuse is never glorified; safety, lawful protection, and nonviolent accountability remain goods.
