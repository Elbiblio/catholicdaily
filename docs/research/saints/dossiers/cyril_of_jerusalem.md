# Saint Cyril of Jerusalem

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `cyril_of_jerusalem`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `cyril_of_jerusalem`, `saint_cyril_of_jerusalem_bishop_and_doctor_of_the_church`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Cyril of Jerusalem; Cyril, Bishop of Jerusalem
- Feast date and calendar scope: March 18; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: Sources give approximately 313-386, about 315-386, and about 315-387. The profile uses `c. 315-late 380s` and no exact age. Cyril's serious conflict with Acacius involved doctrine and jurisdiction. The familiar account of exactly three exiles and an uncomplicated lifelong Nicene alignment is qualified by modern reassessment. The five mystagogical lectures are treated as traditionally associated with Cyril while their possible authorship by his successor John remains open.

## Uncertainties and conflicts

- Approximate date ranges disagree by several years. `c. 315-late 380s` communicates the evidence without a false exact age.
- Benedict XVI reports the traditional three-exile outline while noting conflicting sources; Van Nuffelen argues that several supposed removals under Julian and Valens cannot be demonstrated in the usual form. The profile labels the overall history mixed.
- Cyril's development toward the settlement received at Constantinople is more complex than describing him as a straightforward Nicene champion from the start.
- The five mystagogical lectures remain valuable witnesses within the Cyril corpus while their precise authorship is disputed.
- No formal patronage or stable symbol was established by the source set, so both fields remain empty.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Ancient anti-Jewish, anti-pagan, anti-Manichaean, and anti-heretical invective is neither quoted nor turned into present-day imitation. Truth requires accuracy and respect for persons.
- Catechesis is presented as patient, freely received formation, never coercion, humiliation, or information overload.
- The episcopal conflict is not used to sanctify partisan power or demonize an opponent.
- The practical guide adds accountable support where formation intersects trauma, coercion, or safeguarding.
