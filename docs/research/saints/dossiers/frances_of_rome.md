# Saint Frances of Rome

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `frances_of_rome`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `frances_of_rome`, `saint_frances_of_rome_religious`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Frances of Rome; Francesca Romana; Francesca Bussa de' Ponziani
- Feast date and calendar scope: March 9; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: The profile preserves 1384-1440, marriage, motherhood, service, the 1425 oblation, the 1433 Tor de' Specchi development, and entry into common life after widowhood in 1436. Arranged marriage is historical context, not a norm commanding coerced or unsafe marriage. Visions and guardian-angel accounts are received tradition rather than secure chronology. A 2025 papal message contains impossible references to much later saints accompanying Frances, so those names are rejected.

## Family, consent, and safety review

- The profile does not idealize marriage arranged under family pressure or imply that a woman must accept coercion, abuse, danger, or silenced conscience.
- Charity is bounded by consent, dependents' safety, household resources, privacy, infection control, and competent organizational practice.
- Later common life follows widowhood; it is not used to justify abandoning a spouse or dependent.
- Historical asceticism and contested household-resource stories are not prescribed for imitation.
- Private visions are judged by Christlike fruit and never override Scripture, conscience, duties, wise counsel, or safeguarding.

## Content review

The factual review checked marriage and motherhood, Roman crisis, hospital and poverty service, oblation and approval chronology, non-cloistered women's context, widowhood, later community life, and angel tradition. It records the Odebiyi article's 3 March 2025 online publication date separately from its September 2024 issue assignment. It removed a dead USCCB citation, narrowed epidemic language to documented civic context rather than personal plague treatment, recorded the papal-message anachronism, kept retrospective motorist patronage out of formal metadata without the original instrument, and separated secure history from miraculous or devotional narrative.

## Theological review

Holiness grows through free and responsible response to Christ, never coercion or unsafe submission. Marriage, motherhood, civic charity, widowhood, and religious community are honored as distinct seasons. Prayer and private devotion remain accountable to love in deed and truth, including consent, boundaries, family obligations, and protection of vulnerable people.
