## Sparse history and violence safeguard

- The profile does not invent chronology from later Acts or present the familiar year 303 as certain.
- It does not identify George with Eusebius's unnamed martyr, invent a military rank, or repeat dramatic speeches and tortures.
- The dragon remains a late legend and cannot license violence, invulnerability claims, or the dehumanization of an opponent.
- Martyrdom is faithful endurance of wrongdoing, not a recommendation to seek death or refuse reasonable safety.

## Content review

The factual pass checked the Lydda cult, April 23 reception, unreliability of the Acts, late dragon development, and later English and Garter reception. It rejected the legacy exact year 303, birthplace claims, a detailed Roman military career, identification with Eusebius's unnamed martyr, courtroom speeches, torture sequences, broad national and occupational patronage lists, and treating the dragon as history.

## Theological review

Christ the faithful witness, not a heroic fantasy, is central. Courage is joined to truth, prudence, lawful protection, and refusal to dehumanize. The profile neither glorifies killing nor treats martyrdom as self-harm. Veneration remains compatible with historical restraint because holiness does not depend on an embellished biography.
