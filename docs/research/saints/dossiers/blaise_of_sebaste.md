# Saint Blaise

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `blaise_of_sebaste`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `blaise_of_sebaste`, `saint_blaise_bishop_and_martyr`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Blaise; Saint Blasius
- Feast date and calendar scope: February 3; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: Ancient and medieval sources use forms of Blaise or Blasius. The reviewed historical control supports only the cautious core of a bishop of Sebaste and martyr from the early fourth century. The c. 316 date, medical profession, cave, animals, detailed execution narrative, and throat episode all depend on later tradition to varying degrees, so no exact death date or life length is asserted.

## Copyright and media decision

All biography, reflection, practices, and prayer are original Catholic Daily prose. No liturgical collect, blessing formula, or source passage is reproduced. The throat episode is summarized briefly because its genre and devotional consequence are relevant, not retold for dramatic effect. No image is included because no individual file's creator, source page, license, credit line, and derivative status were reviewed together.

## Content review

The factual pass separated three layers: the cautious historical core of bishop and martyr; the later legendary Acts; and the documented continuation of the throat-blessing custom. It rejected an exact death year, a medical career as fact, a detailed arrest or execution account, animal legends, relic claims, and unsupported patronage. Martyrdom remains non-graphic. The medical safety language was checked to state that prayer and blessing neither diagnose nor treat illness and must not delay emergency help for choking, breathing difficulty, or other dangerous symptoms.

## Theological review

The profile directs prayer to Christ and understands saintly intercession as dependent on God. It avoids magical interpretations of a sacramental and makes no connection between the strength of faith and the outcome of an illness. The suggested Scripture is used to locate Blaise's witness within Christ's mission. Suffering and execution are never called good; courage lies in fidelity, not in the harm inflicted.
