## Copyright and media decision

All three sources were used to verify facts and the Church's interpretation. The biography, reflections, practices, and prayer are original Catholic Daily prose and do not track a source paragraph-by-paragraph. No quotation is included. No image was accepted for revision 1 because an individual file's creator, source page, and license were not researched to the validator's standard; the page is intentionally text-only.

## Content review

The review rechecked the approximate birth year, Darfur origin, loss of her childhood name, chronology of captivity, Venice and the Canossians, the legal recognition of freedom, the date of Christian initiation, profession in 1896, long service at Schio, death in 1947, and canonization in 2000. It removed the legacy patronage word “slave” and replaced it with survivor-centred wording. It also checked that violence is acknowledged accurately without graphic detail and that no act of abuse is called good, necessary, or spiritually beneficial.

## Theological review

The guide keeps Christ at the centre of Bakhita's hope and distinguishes divine love from human ownership. Baptism is not framed as a substitute for legal and bodily freedom. Intercession language is directed through prayer to God, the practical action prioritizes survivor safety and reputable support, and Romans 8 is used to explain hope without denying suffering. The prayer is explicitly app-authored in the UI and asks for protection, healing, justice, and conversion from domination.
