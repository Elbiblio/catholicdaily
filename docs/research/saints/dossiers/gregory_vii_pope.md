## Reform, coercion, and power safeguards

- Canonization recognizes holiness; it does not canonize every proposition in Gregory's register, every sanction, alliance, policy, political judgment, or later interpretation.
- Investiture conflict is not told as a saint defeating a villain. Henry's actions, Gregory's sanctions, episcopal and princely resistance, civil war, the antipope, and disputed interpretations of Canossa remain distinct.
- Excommunication, release from oaths, deposition claims, and medieval coercive politics are not a toolkit for modern family, workplace, parish, or civic leadership.
- Reform consequences include clergy wives and children, Roman civilians harmed in 1084, and communities affected by Gregory's support for Norman and English conquest projects.
- A good institutional aim does not erase coercion or collateral harm. Modern imitation requires evidence, consultation, safeguards, review, and protection for people with less power.
