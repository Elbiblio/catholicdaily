# Saint Gregory of Narek

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `gregory_of_narek`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `gregory_of_narek`, `saint_gregory_of_narek_abbot_and_doctor_of_the_church`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Gregory of Narek; Grigor Narekatsi; Gregory of Nareg
- Feast date and calendar scope: February 27; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: The reviewed sources give incompatible approximate dates: about 950-1003, about 950 to roughly 1005, and about 945-1010. The exact legacy span 951-1003 and an exact age were rejected in favor of `c. 950-early 11th century`. The Roman title is Abbot and Doctor, but Armenian and scholarly accounts securely describe monk, priest, teacher, and writer without establishing a term governing Narek. The canonical title is retained without inventing an abbacy narrative.

## Uncertainties and conflicts

- Date estimates range from about 945-1010. The profile uses `c. 950-early 11th century`, leaves life length blank, and marks historical certainty mixed.
- The title Abbot is authoritative in the Roman liturgy; reviewed Armenian and scholarly narratives do not establish administrative detail, so no governing role is inferred.
- Traditions of miracles and healing belong to devotional reception but are unnecessary to the profile and are not presented as medical evidence.
- No formal patronage or stable symbols were established by the source set; both fields remain empty.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Compunction is joined explicitly to Christ's mercy, hope, proportionate repair, and communion. It is not self-loathing, scrupulosity, or personal guilt for another person's wrongdoing.
- No punitive fasting or harmful bodily discipline is proposed. Prayer is not a substitute for medical or psychological care.
- The practice includes a stop condition and directs a reader in serious distress toward trusted pastoral and qualified mental-health or emergency support.
- Gregory remains an Armenian Christian teacher; universal reception and the Doctor title do not convert his biography into a Roman-Catholic assimilation story.

## Theological review

- Sin and woundedness are neither denied nor made the final word; Christ's saving mercy and timely grace remain primary.
- Intercession expands personal lament toward neighbor-love without confusing solidarity with false personal guilt.
- The profile respects the Armenian Church's role in preserving Gregory's witness and frames Catholic reception ecumenically.
- The Doctor title is stated from the formal 2015 papal source without erasing Gregory's original ecclesial home.
- The prayer asks for justice and reconciliation, not forgetfulness or cheap peace.
