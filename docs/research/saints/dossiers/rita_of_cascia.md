## Marriage, family, and miracle safeguards

- Rita's memory never normalizes violence, tells a victim to remain in an unsafe marriage, or equates boundaries and emergency help with failed forgiveness.
- A family member's revenge is not under another person's spiritual control. Prayer does not guarantee changed behavior, survival, reconciliation, fertility, healing, or another requested outcome.
- Forgiveness rejects retaliation but does not restore access automatically or replace criminal justice, safeguarding, medical care, legal counsel, or domestic-violence support.
- Refusing available help is not heroic. The wound is reported as received devotional tradition and is never a model for self-injury.
- The rose and miracle traditions are not used as historical controls. A contemporary casket or devotional artifact could document reception only, never prove a miracle.

## Theological review

Christ's peace is separated from passive exposure to violence. Forgiveness is compatible with truth, boundaries, justice, and protection. Prayer and intercession are not techniques for controlling people or outcomes. Suffering and bodily injury are never made intrinsically holy, and Rita's later vocation does not portray marriage or motherhood as inferior states.
