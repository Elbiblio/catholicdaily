- Feast date and calendar scope: March 8; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: Tradition gives about 1495 and Montemor-o-Novo, but exact birth date and birthplace are not equally secure, so the profile uses `c. 1495-1550` and lists only Granada as a place. ## Mental-health and safeguarding review

- The text describes observable conduct and documented treatment without diagnosing bipolar disorder, psychosis, schizophrenia, or any other modern condition.
- It does not repeat historical slurs for mental illness and does not claim suffering cured or sanctified John.
- Self-harm, crowd violence, shackling, flogging, stripping, confinement, and neglect are condemned without graphic detail.
- Anyone in acute crisis or immediate danger is routed to local emergency or crisis services.
- Service occurs only through verified organizations with screening, training, supervision, confidentiality, safeguarding, and role boundaries. Unsupervised access and amateur clinical advice are explicitly prohibited.

## Copyright and media decision

The profile paraphrases the primary biography and letter in original prose. It reproduces no prayer or long passage. No image is included because file-specific licensing was not researched.

## Content review

The factual review separated early Castro evidence, John's own letter, source-critical analysis, independent reference control, and posthumous Order history. It checked the crisis, institutional abuse, practical hospital system, companions, canonical chronology, formal patronage, and later legends. It removed retrospective diagnosis, miracle-centered storytelling, inflated claims that John invented modern psychiatry, and unsafe lone-rescuer language.

## Theological review

Christ is presented as compassionate healer and source of charity, not as a replacement for clinicians or evidence-based care. Grace meets people in suffering without making abuse good or necessary. Mercy respects dignity, consent, agency, professional competence, and accountable institutions. The prayer explicitly joins spiritual care to treatment and social support.
