- Feast date and calendar scope: February 11; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar and annual World Day of the Sick
- Identity conflicts resolved: This is a Marian title and apparition observance, not a separate human biography of Mary. Empty lifespan fields are deliberate. The Immaculate Conception concerns Mary's own conception by grace through Christ's merits; it is not the virginal conception of Jesus. Bernadette's reported apparitions, the 1862 diocesan recognition, devotional pilgrimage, and claims of physical healing are treated as distinct evidence categories.

## Copyright and media decision

All prose and prayer are original. The profile does not reproduce the diocesan decree, reported apparition words, a liturgical collect, or a pilgrim testimony. It uses only the minimum paraphrase necessary to identify doctrine and the reported events. No image is included because no individual Lourdes or Marian file was verified for creator, source page, license, credit line, and permitted derivatives.

## Content review

The factual pass separately checked the Catholic doctrine defined in 1854, Bernadette's 1858 reports and their dates, the commission of inquiry, Bishop Laurence's 1862 recognition, the growth of pilgrimage, the February 11 World Day of the Sick, and the Medical Bureau's structured process. It removed mutable cure totals, unreviewed miracle examples, claims about Lourdes water, broad geographic patronage, and any promise of healing. Empty lifespan fields were retained because this is an observance, not an additional stage in Mary's earthly life.

## Theological review

Christ's merits ground the Immaculate Conception, and Christ's death and resurrection remain the source of salvation and hope. Mary intercedes and models discipleship; she neither replaces Jesus nor controls clinical outcomes. The page affirms prayer for bodily healing while refusing presumption, blame, or the idea that disability and illness reveal weak faith. Care, accessible medicine, and faithful presence are presented as Christian responsibilities.
