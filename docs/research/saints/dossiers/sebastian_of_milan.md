## Content review

The content review explicitly separates securely documented history from early and later tradition. Secure: the name, martyr identity, January 20 commemoration, and Appian Way location appear in the fourth-century Depositio; an ancient Milan connection comes through Ambrose. Later and legendary: military rank, palace access, prisoner ministry, arrows, Irene, and a second execution derive from the fifth-century Passio. The profile removes the legacy `255-287` dates, gives no graphic account, and does not use Renaissance iconography as historical evidence.

## Theological review

The guide honors martyrdom as faithful witness to Christ while condemning persecution as evil. It does not imply that injury, execution, or refusal of prudent safety is spiritually desirable. The traditional solidarity motif supports careful help for endangered people, with explicit attention to security. Scripture and prayer center on Christ's acknowledgment of faithful disciples, hope of resurrection, protection of the persecuted, and conversion of those who abuse power.
