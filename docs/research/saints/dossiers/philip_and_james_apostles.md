## Joint-celebration safeguard

The profile uses `group`, preserves both celebration aliases, writes both names in headings and prayer, and refuses a shared lifespan. Unity comes from Christ's call and the Church's celebration, not from identical biographies. Pastoral applications invite distinct participation and protect quieter contributions from absorption into a more visible person's story.

## Philip identity and tradition safeguard

Philip's profile is limited to the Johannine apostle and the apostle-list tradition. The guide retains his call, invitation to Nathanael, bread question, role with Greek visitors, and request to see the Father. Later places and death methods are marked as variable and omitted from top-level places. Philip the deacon is not used to extend the apostle's biography.

## James identity safeguard

The secure core is intentionally small: James son of Alphaeus belongs to the Twelve. Benedict XVI explicitly acknowledges the debated relationship with James the Lord's brother, while the specialist reference distinguishes them in modern scholarship. Therefore Jerusalem leadership, authorship of the Letter of James, the AD 62 death account, later relic sites, and a fuller's club do not become this apostle's certain facts. Sparse evidence is handled as a limit, not an invitation to invention.

## Theological review

James's obscurity supports faithful belonging without invention. The practice protects freedom in invitation and honors quiet work.
