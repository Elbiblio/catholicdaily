# Saint Patrick

## Identity resolution

- Stable ID: `patrick_of_ireland`
- Profile kind: individual
- Celebration IDs: `patrick_of_ireland`, `saint_patrick_bishop`
- Canonical name and aliases: Saint Patrick; Patricius; Pádraig
- Feast date and calendar scope: March 17; optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar and solemnity in Ireland
- Identity conflicts resolved: Patrick's Confessio and Letter to Coroticus control the profile. They establish Romano-British origin, capture at about sixteen, six years of enslavement, escape, later episcopal mission, and opposition to slave raiding. Exact birthplace and dates remain debated, so the legacy fifth-century span is preserved. Earlier Christianity and Palladius prevent a single-founder narrative. Snakes and shamrock accounts are later traditions.

## Mission, captivity, and safeguarding review

- Enslavement, trafficking, killing, captivity, and forced displacement are condemned without graphic detail.
- Mission respects freedom, questions, local knowledge, culture, and a person's right to decline without losing aid or dignity.
- The action is locally led, safeguarding-compliant, and survivor-centred. It prohibits trading aid for religious participation and prohibits lone confrontation of suspected traffickers.
- Patrick is not appropriated for nationalist triumph or used to erase prior Christians, Palladius, local converts, women, or clergy.
- Primary translations are paraphrased rather than reproduced.

## Copyright and media decision

The profile uses original prose and short source concepts rather than copied translation. It quotes no hymn, collect, or primary passage. No image is included because file-specific licensing was not researched.

## Content review

The factual pass checked both primary writings, their different purposes, capture and escape, episcopal mission, Coroticus, chronology debate, earlier Christianity, formal Irish patronage, and the late dates of the snakes and shamrock traditions. It removed exact dates, nationalist conquest, coercive mission, legacy snake patronage, engineers, and any claim that suffering was spiritually beneficial.

## Theological review

Christ's love, not Patrick's heroism or a nation, is the source of mission. Witness is humble, non-coercive, locally accountable, and inseparable from defense of human freedom. Grace can redeem a survivor's future without endorsing the evil endured. The prayer condemns religious coercion and directs Irish patronage toward justice, welcome, and peace.
