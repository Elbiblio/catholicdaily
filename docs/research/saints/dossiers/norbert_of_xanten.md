## Uncertainties and conflicts

- Birth chronology varies. The profile preserves a range and does not calculate an age.
- Vita A and Vita B are posthumous hagiographic sources of uncertain authorship and date. They preserve early evidence but arrange it for spiritual and institutional purposes.
- The reported lightning or storm conversion scene is labeled as hagiographic tradition. Spider, demon, assassination, and sensational Tanchelm stories are excluded.
- Norbert's anti-heresy and political setting cannot authorize coercion, contempt, state domination, or the claim that every episcopal tactic was holy.
- The later Eucharistic title is received devotion, not proof of every Eucharistic legend or medieval attribution.

## Pastoral safety notes

- Conversion means accountable review and repair, not seeking frightening experiences or making impulsive vows.
- Medieval barefoot travel, exposure, fasting, sleep loss, or severe penance are not imitation prompts. Health, disability, dependants, safeguarding, and competent advice take priority.
- Obedience never removes conscience, due process, reporting duties, or safeguards against spiritual abuse.
- Reform should include listening, evidence, shared authority, review, and protection for people affected by decisions.
- Disagreement about doctrine or discipline never licenses dehumanization or force.

## Theological review

- Christ calls conversion toward communion, service, and repair rather than personality cult.
- Eucharistic devotion is connected to ecclesial community and care for people, not triumphalism.
- Reform honors Church authority while requiring prudence, due process, and moral limits on power.
- Human suffering and unsafe austerity are never treated as spiritual achievements.
