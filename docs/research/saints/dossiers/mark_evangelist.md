- Feast date and calendar scope: April 25; Feast in the General Roman Calendar
- Identity conflicts resolved: The canonical Gospel is internally anonymous apart from its ancient heading. Papias and Irenaeus are early reception evidence for a Mark linked to Peter, not automatic modern proof that the writer was the John Mark of every New Testament reference. Because the honored evangelist is not directly identified inside Scripture, `individual` is safer than the schema's `biblical` kind, which is defined for figures primarily known through Scripture. Rome is traditional and possible. Alexandria is a living later ecclesial tradition; exact foundation dates and the detailed AD 68 martyr story are not established biography. Top-level places remain empty so unqualified chips do not overstate either association.

## Authorship, Alexandria, martyrdom, and iconography safeguard

- The canonical Gospel is not described as internally signed by Mark.
- Traditional attribution, possible John Mark identity, Peter association, and Roman setting are distinguished rather than fused into one certain biography.
- Alexandrian foundation and martyr memory are treated as a living Coptic ecclesial tradition with later and variable chronology.
- AD 68, dragging mechanics, and other apocryphal death details are not repeated as history.
- The winged lion is material and textual reception evidence, not a creature attached to Mark's first-century life.

## Theological review

Jesus Christ and the canonical Gospel remain central. Historical qualification does not disparage Scripture or the Coptic Church, and veneration does not require pretending debated identity questions are settled. Discipleship is cross-shaped, open to correction, and free of triumphalism. The prayer asks for attentive reception and humble proclamation rather than certainty about later details.
