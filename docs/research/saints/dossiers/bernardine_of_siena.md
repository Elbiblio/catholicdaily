## Anti-Judaism, gender, and coercion safeguard

- Jewish people are neighbors and historical subjects, never a rhetorical category for greed, unbelief, or civic danger.
- Bernardine's anti-Jewish demands and their coercive effects are named plainly. Slurs, hostile analogies, and contemptuous sermon language are not reused.
- The profile does not ask Jewish readers to reconcile the saint's reputation with harms done to Jewish communities, or imply that canonization resolves the moral record.
- Primary gender rhetoric is acknowledged without reproducing it as marriage or pastoral advice.
- Concern about usury is separated from stereotypes about Jewish lenders; economic justice is applied without ethnic or religious scapegoating.
