## Copyright and media decision

All wording is original. The profile does not reproduce Cyprian's letter, Eusebius's translated narrative, or a liturgical collect. No image is used because file-specific rights, creator, and credit information were not documented.

## Content review

The content review explicitly separates secure history from tradition. Securely documented: Fabian was bishop of Rome from 236, his administration was respected by Cyprian, and he died in the Decian persecution in 250. Reliably traditional: Eusebius's fourth-century dove account of his unexpected election. Later-record material: the seven-district organization and cemetery works. The profile omits the legacy birth year and exact age, avoids a guessed method of execution, and treats persecution without graphic language.

## Theological review

The guide locates Fabian's courage in fidelity to Christ and pastoral responsibility, not in violence done to him. His death is never called good; the good named is his refusal to abandon the people and faith entrusted to him. The practices emphasize prayer, prudent support for endangered Christians, and accountable leadership. The prayer asks Christ to protect the persecuted and convert persecutors without romanticizing danger.
